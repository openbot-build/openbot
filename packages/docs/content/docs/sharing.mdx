---
title: Share Bots
description:
  Invite other people to use one of your Bots in their own organization, by
  email or with a link.
---

You can share a Bot with people outside your organization, like you add a member
to an organization. You invite a person by email, or you make an **invitation
link**. Your client can show the link as a QR code. The person accepts the
invitation and then uses the Bot in their own organization.

| Word            | Meaning                                                                                         |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shared Bot      | Your Bot that other people use.                                                                 |
| Invitation link | An invitation without an email. Everyone who has its secret can accept it, until you revoke it. |
| Bot member      | A person who accepted an invitation and uses the Bot.                                           |
| Linked Bot      | The Bot that a member gets in their own organization. It uses your Bot.                         |

---

## Invite people

You need `bots.share` on the Bot. Members can share their own Bots.
Administrators can also share the shared Bots of the organization. Only a top
Bot can be shared: one that a member or the organization owns, and that is not
archived.

To invite one person, send their email. The invitation lasts 7 days, like an
[invitation to the organization](/docs/organization#invitations):

```sh
curl -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $OPENBOT_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"email":"ana@example.com"}' \
  https://api.example.com/v1/bots/$BOT/invitations
```

To make an invitation link, leave out `email`. Many people can use the same
link.

```sh
curl -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $OPENBOT_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"path":"/join"}' \
  https://api.example.com/v1/bots/$BOT/invitations
```

| Field       | Meaning                                                                      |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `email`     | The email of the person. Leave it out to make a link.                        |
| `expiresAt` | When a link stops (milliseconds since 1970). Without it, a link never stops. |
| `path`      | A path on the public origin where your client opens invitation links.        |

```json
{
	"invitation": {
		"id": "inv_51c2…",
		"bot": "bot_7d2c",
		"email": null,
		"prefix": "obai_Xk2pQ9aB",
		"path": "/join",
		"status": "pending",
		"invitedBy": "u_123",
		"createdAt": "2026-10-23T08:00:00.000Z",
		"expiresAt": null
	},
	"secret": "obai_Xk2pQ9aB…",
	"url": "https://api.example.com/join#obai_Xk2pQ9aB…"
}
```

`url` is the public origin, then `path`, then `#` and the secret. A browser
never sends the part after `#` to a server, so the secret never goes into a
server log. `url` is `null` when there is no `path`. Show `url` as a QR code, or
build your own address from `secret`.

`GET /v1/bots/:botId/invitations/:invitationId/secret` gives the secret and the
`url` of a link again, for example to print the QR code again. OpenBot keeps the
secret sealed, and keeps a hash to find the link.

`GET /v1/bots/:botId/invitations` lists the invitations.
`DELETE /v1/bots/:botId/invitations/:invitationId` revokes one. After that,
nobody can accept it. The people who accepted it before stay members.

## Accept an invitation

The invited person signs in. They use the session cookie or their own user
token.

- An email invitation shows in `GET /v1/invitations`, with `bot`. Accept it with
  its ID.
- For a link, send its secret.

```sh
curl -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $THEIR_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"secret":"obai_Xk2pQ9aB…","organization":"org_9a1f"}' \
  https://api.example.com/v1/bot-invitations/accept
```

`organization` is the organization where the person uses the Bot. They must be
an active member of it. They can leave it out when they have only one
organization, or when they use a user token. The answer is their new linked Bot.

When the person already uses the Bot, they get their linked Bot back. To say no
to an email invitation, use `POST /v1/invitations/:invitationId/decline`.

## Use a shared Bot

The linked Bot is a Bot of the member, in their own organization. It has
`source`, which names your Bot:

```json
{
	"id": "bot_e40b",
	"owner": { "_tag": "Member", "userId": "u_456" },
	"name": "Guide",
	"source": { "organizationId": "org_3c7e", "bot": "bot_7d2c" }
}
```

The member works with it like with their own Bots:

- They talk to it in their own [Chat](/docs/chats), in their own organization,
  and can make it their default Bot.
- It works with their permissions, their connected [Apps](/docs/apps), and their
  other Bots. It can give work to their Bots.
- Its [requests](/docs/requests) go to the member. Their decisions,
  [Grants](/docs/requests#answer-a-request) and [Rules](/docs/rules) count only
  in their organization. Yours count only in yours.
- Their organization pays for the models. When your Bot names a model that their
  organization does not have, their Chat does not use it. It uses the default
  model of the server, when the server has one.
- You never see their Chats.

The name, role, instructions and model always come from your Bot. When you
change them, every linked Bot changes too. A member cannot change them. A member
can change `planApproval`.

The Bot keeps [one memory pool](/docs/memory), in your organization. What it
learns in the organization of a member is for that member, in that organization.
Nobody reads the memory about another person: not you, and not other members.
You can remove what a member left with a purge, without reading it. Only you
manage the memory Bot.

## Manage members

| Request                                  | What it does                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `GET /v1/bots/:botId/members`            | Lists the members: their user ID and organization, and their linked Bot. |
| `DELETE /v1/bots/:botId/members/:userId` | Takes a person off. Their linked Bot stops working at once.              |

Both need `bots.share`. A linked Bot also stops when you archive your Bot.

A member leaves when they archive their linked Bot.

## Errors

| Status | Error                   | When                                                                             |
| ------ | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `400`  | `BotInvalid`            | The Bot cannot be shared, or a member tries to change what the shared Bot is.    |
| `400`  | `InvitationInvalid`     | The invitation is used, revoked or expired, or you must name the organization.   |
| `404`  | `InvitationNotFound`    | There is no invitation with this ID or secret for you.                           |
| `404`  | `BotNotFound`           | There is no Bot with this ID that you may read, or a linked Bot stopped working. |
| `503`  | `BotStorageUnavailable` | OpenBot cannot reach its database.                                               |
