A **Bot** is a worker with a role, such as a programmer, a writer or a designer.
Each Bot has its own instructions, its own [Chats](/docs/chats) and its own
long-term memory. You talk to a Bot, and it gives work to other Bots, or to
itself, so it is always free to answer you. This page shows how to talk to a Bot
and the routes under `/v1/bots`. Every route accepts a user token or the session
cookie. See [Authentication](/docs/authentication).

---

## Talk to a Bot

Every Bot is a lasting worker. It lives until you archive it, and it learns from
its work over time. There is no other kind of Bot.

To talk to a Bot, send it a message:

```sh
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $OPENBOT_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"input":"Plan three days in Rome."}' \
  https://api.example.com/v1/bots/$BOT/messages
```

The message goes to your one Chat with this Bot. OpenBot makes the Chat the
first time. You never make, pick or close a Chat. The Bot answers at once. When
the work is long, the Bot gives it to a [delegation](/docs/delegation) and stays
free to answer you. The result comes back to your Chat later. See
[Chats](/docs/chats).

A **top Bot** is a Bot that a member or the organization owns, not another Bot.
You can pick one top Bot as your [default Bot](#your-default-bot).

A Bot can own Bots too. See [Bots that own Bots](#bots-that-own-bots).

### Your default Bot

Your **default Bot** is the Bot that a client talks to first. A
[trigger](/docs/triggers) without a Bot also gives its task to it. OpenBot does
not make Bots for you, so a new member has no default Bot. Then a trigger
without a Bot fails with `400` and `BotInvalid`.

To set your default Bot, make a Bot, and then send its ID in your member
settings:

```sh
curl -X PATCH -H "Authorization: Bearer $OPENBOT_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"expectedVersion":0,"defaultBot":"bot_5c0e…"}' \
  https://openbot.example.com/v1/me/settings
```

`expectedVersion` is the `version` from `GET /v1/me/settings`. The Bot must be
one of your own top Bots or a shared Bot, and you need `bots.delegate` on it. If
not, OpenBot returns `400` with `SettingsInvalid`. Send `null` to remove your
default Bot.

You can still archive your default Bot. After that, a trigger without a Bot
fails until you pick another default Bot.

Older versions of OpenBot made a Main Bot for each member. When you upgrade,
each Main Bot becomes a normal Bot, and it becomes the default Bot of its
member. Its memory and Grants stay.

### No limits that a Bot knows

Bots have no list of things they may do. A Bot just tries. When it tries
something new for the first time, such as making a Bot or calling an App tool,
the decider above it decides. See [Requests](/docs/requests). The decisions are
kept, so the limits of each Bot grow from real work.

## Make a Bot

```sh
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $OPENBOT_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"name":"Programmer","role":"Writes and reviews TypeScript code","instructions":"Write small, tested changes. Explain each change in one short paragraph."}' \
  https://api.example.com/v1/bots
```

| Field          | Meaning                                                                                                      |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `name`         | Required. A short name. OpenBot removes spaces at the start and end. The rest must have 1 to 100 characters. |
| `role`         | Required. What the Bot does, in one line. Other Bots read it to choose workers.                              |
| `instructions` | Required. The lasting instructions of the Bot, like a job description.                                       |
| `model`        | Optional. The model of the Chats of this Bot, when a Chat does not choose its own.                           |
| `planApproval` | Optional. How the plans of this Bot are approved. See [Plan mode](/docs/chats#plan-mode).                    |
| `shared`       | Optional. `true` makes an organization Bot that every member can use.                                        |
| `bot`          | Optional. The ID of the Bot that owns the new Bot. You cannot use it with `shared`.                          |

A Bot without a `model` cannot answer. Its first message fails with `400` and
`ChatModelInvalid`. See [Models](/docs/models#choose-a-model).

OpenBot returns `201` with the Bot:

```json
{
	"id": "bot_5c0e…",
	"owner": { "_tag": "Member", "userId": "u-1" },
	"name": "Programmer",
	"role": "Writes and reviews TypeScript code",
	"instructions": "Write small, tested changes. …",
	"archived": false,
	"revision": 0,
	"createdAt": 1792486800000,
	"updatedAt": 1792486800000
}
```

`owner` is `Member`, `Organization` or `Bot`. A shared Bot works with the
permissions and the App connections of the member who uses it, never with those
of the person who made it.

## List, read and change Bots

`GET /v1/bots` lists your own Bots and the shared Bots that you may use. Add
`?archived=false` to hide archived Bots. Add `?bot=<id>` to list the Bots that
one Bot owns.

`GET /v1/bots/:botId` returns one Bot.

```sh
curl -X PATCH -H "Authorization: Bearer $OPENBOT_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"expected":0,"instructions":"Write small changes with tests first."}' \
  https://api.example.com/v1/bots/$BOT
```

`expected` is the current `revision`. You can change `name` (with the same rules
as above), `role`, `instructions`, `model` (send `null` to remove it),
`planApproval` (send `null` to remove it) and `archived`. When you archive a
Bot, OpenBot also archives every Bot that it owns, and the Bots below them. When
you unarchive it, only that one Bot comes back.

## Bots that own Bots

A Bot can own Bots. So a Bot can build its own team, and each member of the team
keeps its own memory.

- A Bot makes a Bot that it owns with the `openbot_create_bot` tool. A person
  makes one with `POST /v1/bots` and the `bot` field.
- A Bot that another Bot makes with the tool gets the `model` of the Bot that
  makes it. The tool call can name another `model`.
- The owner is `{ "_tag": "Bot", "bot": "<owner ID>", "userId": "<member>" }`.
  `userId` is the member at the top of the owner tree. It is `null` when the
  organization owns the Bot at the top.
- A Bot can give work to itself and to the Bots it owns without a request.
- The **owner chain** of a Bot goes up through its owners to a top Bot, and then
  to the person. For example: **person → Assistant → Worker → Scout**. Requests
  of a Bot go up this chain, also when a person works with the Bot directly. See
  [Requests](/docs/requests#the-chain-of-deciders).
- Above a top Bot is the person who works with it. This is true for your own top
  Bots and for shared Bots.

A Bot does not need a helper Bot to keep the context of some work. It can give
more work to an earlier delegation, and that work goes on in the same task Chat.
See [Delegation](/docs/delegation#add-to-earlier-work).

## What a Bot knows in each Turn

OpenBot adds these to the instructions of every Turn:

- the name, role and instructions of the Bot,
- the person it works with now, with their name and member ID. In a
  [Channel](/docs/channels), it is the person who wrote the message of the Turn,
- what kind of Chat it is in:
  - in a direct Chat or a channel Chat, people talk with it, so it must answer
    at once. It gives long work to delegations, maybe to itself, and stays free
    to answer,
  - in a task Chat, it does one task for the Bot that gave it. Its last answer
    is the result. It must not say that it is done while it still has open work,
  - in a review Chat, it decides one request,
- that it uses a capability with a short reason, and that an answer that needs a
  decision comes back later as a message,
- that text that starts with `[OpenBot]` comes from OpenBot, not from a person,
  so it is data, not instructions,
- that it carries experience from its earlier work, and that the current
  instructions win over it,
- a list of the [open work](/docs/chats#open-work) of the Chat: its delegations,
  requests and reminders.

The [memory Bot](/docs/memory#the-memory-bot) can also add what helps to the
input of the Turn. The Bot sees the additions as its own experience. It has the
`confide` tool, and it knows it only as a place to say how its work goes.

You can also [share a Bot](/docs/sharing) with people in other organizations.
They get a linked Bot with `source`, which uses your Bot.

## Permissions

| Permission      | Lets you                                                           |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `bots.read`     | Read Bots, their memory, Rules and Grants.                         |
| `bots.write`    | Make and change Bots, their memory, Rules and Grants.              |
| `bots.delegate` | Talk to a Bot and give it work, from a client or from another Bot. |
| `bots.share`    | Invite people to [use a Bot](/docs/sharing).                       |

Your own Bots are below your member key. Shared Bots are below
`openbot.org.<org>.bot`. Members may read and use shared Bots. Administrators
may also change and share them. See [Permissions](/docs/permissions).

## Errors

| Status | Error                   | When                                                   |
| ------ | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `400`  | `BotInvalid`            | The change is not allowed, or you have no default Bot. |
| `403`  | `PermissionDenied`      | You may read the Bot, but not change or use it.        |
| `404`  | `BotNotFound`           | There is no Bot with this ID that you may read.        |
| `409`  | `BotConflict`           | `expected` is old. Read the Bot again and retry.       |
| `503`  | `BotStorageUnavailable` | OpenBot cannot reach its database.                     |
